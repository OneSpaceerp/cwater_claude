# C-Water Next-Generation Website
## Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Product:** C-Water Corporate & Solutions Website  
**Primary Market:** Egypt, designed for regional expansion across GCC/Africa  
**Languages:** Arabic + English  
**Primary Objective:** Build a premium, futuristic, technically credible B2B website that establishes C-Water as an integrated water-treatment technology partner and generates qualified commercial opportunities.  
**Brand Direction:** Existing C-Water logo and color palette retained; visual language substantially redesigned.  
**Partner Ecosystem:** Walchem + TIMEX + Kurita Europe, with C-Water remaining the master brand.

---

# 1. Overview

## 1.1 Product Vision

The new C-Water website will transform C-Water's digital presence from a traditional water-treatment company website into a **high-end digital experience for industrial and commercial water-treatment solutions**.

The website must communicate one unified proposition:

> **C-Water combines local engineering expertise with international water-treatment technologies to design, control, optimize, and maintain water systems throughout their lifecycle.**

The experience will combine the strongest characteristics identified across C-Water, Walchem, TIMEX, and Kurita:

- C-Water's local engineering, consultancy, analysis, technical support, and after-sales capability.
- Walchem's instrumentation, dosing, sensing, control, automation, and connected monitoring capabilities.
- TIMEX's filtration engineering and broad industrial filtration portfolio.
- Kurita's advanced chemistry, application engineering, optimization, sustainability, and measurable operational outcomes.

C-Water must remain clearly recognizable as the solution provider. Partner technologies must strengthen credibility rather than fragment the brand.

The existing C-Water website currently communicates general water-treatment products, equipment, and chemicals, but the new product must elevate the brand from a product supplier to an **integrated engineering and water-management partner**.

---

## 1.2 Product Positioning

The website shall position C-Water around five strategic pillars:

**Engineering**  
Design solutions around real operating conditions and customer requirements.

**Technology**  
Deploy internationally recognized filtration, chemical-treatment, sensing, dosing, and control technologies.

**Intelligence**  
Use monitoring, measurement, automation, reporting, and data to improve operational decisions.

**Service**  
Support customers beyond initial equipment or chemical supply.

**Results**  
Connect treatment programs to reliability, efficiency, cost, water consumption, energy consumption, equipment life, and operational continuity.

The resulting message should be closer to:

> **Advanced Water Treatment. Engineered Around Your Operation.**

than a conventional product-sales proposition.

---

## 1.3 Experience Concept

The digital experience should be based on the concept:

### **From Water Problem → Engineering Insight → Integrated Solution → Measurable Result**

The website should continually answer four questions:

1. **What problem does the customer have?**
2. **Why is C-Water qualified to solve it?**
3. **What technologies and services make up the solution?**
4. **What business/operational outcome can the customer achieve?**

---

## 1.4 Core Experience Principles

### Principle 1 — Customer problem before product

Users should be able to start with their challenge or application instead of needing to know which product they need.

### Principle 2 — Progressive technical depth

The website should work for both:

- executive/commercial users who need a clear business proposition;
- engineers/procurement teams who need detailed technical information.

The interface should reveal technical depth progressively rather than overwhelming users on first entry.

### Principle 3 — C-Water first

C-Water is the primary brand, solution architect, and relationship owner.

Partner brands must appear as technology credentials and solution components.

### Principle 4 — Evidence over claims

Case studies, technical documentation, certifications, specifications, measured results, project examples, and application knowledge should be used wherever available.

### Principle 5 — Motion with purpose

Animations should explain systems, relationships, processes, technology, and outcomes.

Motion must not exist purely for visual decoration.

### Principle 6 — Conversion without feeling transactional

The site must generate RFQs and qualified leads without looking like an e-commerce catalog.

# 2. Problem Statement

## 2.1 Current Problems

The existing website communicates C-Water's presence in water treatment, but does not fully communicate the scale, technical depth, partner ecosystem, and engineering value of the business. Its current experience is largely centered on broad product/service categories rather than complete application journeys.

The new product must solve the following problems.

### Problem A — Fragmented proposition

Customers may perceive:

- chemicals as one business;
- filtration as another;
- controllers/instrumentation as another;
- water-treatment systems as another.

The new website must present these as one connected C-Water capability.

### Problem B — Product-first navigation

A customer may know the problem but not the technology or exact product required.

The new website must support problem-led discovery.

### Problem C — Insufficient technical storytelling

Industrial engineers need to understand:

- application;
- treatment objective;
- technology;
- operating conditions;
- product specifications;
- documentation;
- service;
- implementation/support.

The site must make this technical journey easy.

### Problem D — Insufficient executive/business storytelling

Decision makers need to understand:

- operational risk;
- cost;
- reliability;
- energy;
- water consumption;
- asset life;
- downtime;
- sustainability.

The website must communicate the business value of technical solutions.

Kurita's current digital proposition demonstrates the value of connecting water-treatment technology to reliability, efficiency, asset lifetime, production, water use, energy use, and cost.

### Problem E — Partner brands are not converted into one capability

Walchem, TIMEX, and Kurita individually provide significant capabilities.

Walchem's portfolio connects controllers, sensors, chemical feed, monitoring and remote operational visibility.

TIMEX spans many filtration technologies and application areas, including self-cleaning filtration, cartridge filtration, DAF, disc filters, water-treatment systems, industrial applications, oil & gas, municipal use, irrigation, and aquaculture.

Kurita covers broad water-treatment applications including boiler, cooling/process water, membrane treatment, wastewater, drinking water, cleaning, and other specialized applications.

C-Water must convert this breadth into a simple customer-facing proposition.

### Problem F — Weak lead qualification

A generic “Contact Us” journey does not sufficiently qualify opportunities.

The new site must collect useful technical and commercial context before or during an RFQ journey.

### Problem G — Limited proof of expertise

The site must become an evidence-rich environment containing:

- projects;
- case studies;
- partner technologies;
- technical resources;
- FAQs;
- application knowledge;
- certifications;
- measurable outcomes;
- customer references where permitted.

# 3. Goals and Non-Goals

## 3.1 Goals

### G1 — Establish C-Water as a premium technical authority

A first-time visitor should quickly understand that C-Water is capable of designing and supporting complete water-treatment programs rather than simply reselling individual products.

### G2 — Create a unified C-Water technology ecosystem

The website must visibly connect:

**Chemistry + Filtration + Dosing + Sensing + Control + Treatment Systems + Analysis + Engineering + Service**

into integrated solutions.

### G3 — Generate qualified leads

Primary conversion actions:

- Request a Quote
- Request a Site Assessment
- Discuss a Water Problem
- Request Technical Information
- Contact an Engineer

### G4 — Support technical buyers

Engineers and procurement users must be able to locate:

- product specifications;
- technical descriptions;
- datasheets;
- application information;
- relevant products;
- related technologies;
- downloadable documents.

### G5 — Support non-technical decision makers

The site must explain complex technologies through business outcomes and clear language.

### G6 — Establish a scalable content architecture

The website must support future growth in:

- products;
- industries;
- applications;
- partner technologies;
- projects;
- articles;
- technical documents;
- services;
- geographic markets.

### G7 — Deliver a premium bilingual experience

English and Arabic must be first-class experiences rather than one language being a translated afterthought.

### G8 — Create a modern digital brand expression

The website must feel:

**premium + futuristic + industrial + credible + intelligent + human**

rather than:

**generic + corporate + template-based + catalog-heavy**.

---

## 3.2 Non-Goals

The website will not initially attempt to:

- become a transactional e-commerce store;
- replace ERP/CRM systems;
- provide unrestricted access to proprietary engineering calculations;
- create a public self-service water-treatment design tool that replaces engineering review;
- represent partners as independent companies competing with C-Water;
- publish unsupported performance claims;
- create unverified “savings calculators” using arbitrary assumptions.

# 7. Constraints and Assumptions

## 7.1 Brand Constraint

The existing C-Water logo and primary brand color palette must be retained.

The visual system around the identity may be redesigned substantially.

---

## 7.2 Partner Branding Constraint

Walchem, TIMEX, and Kurita branding must be represented accurately and within permitted partnership/licensing guidelines.

The site shall not imply ownership of partner intellectual property.

---

## 7.3 Content Accuracy

Technical specifications shall come from approved C-Water or partner sources.

No invented:

- certifications;
- capacities;
- product specifications;
- performance guarantees;
- customer results;
- project references.

---

## 7.4 Commercial Accuracy

The website may describe capabilities, but commercial availability must remain aligned with C-Water's actual distribution/partnership arrangements.

---

## 7.5 Regional Strategy

The initial commercial focus is Egypt.

The information architecture shall nevertheless support future regional expansion without requiring a complete redesign.

---

## 7.6 Bilingual Requirement

English and Arabic are required from the initial product release.

---

## 7.7 Catalog Assumption

The product experience is a **technical catalog with RFQ functionality**, not transactional e-commerce.

---

## 7.8 Content Assumption

The site should launch with a deliberately curated set of high-quality content rather than attempting to migrate every existing page blindly.

Existing content should be:

**Keep → Rewrite → Consolidate → Remove**

rather than simply copy/paste into the new site.

---

## 7.9 Technical Content Assumption

Some product specifications and technical information will originate from partner documentation and must be reviewed and approved before being published under the C-Water website.

---

# 8. Out of Scope

The initial website will not include:

- full e-commerce checkout;
- online payment;
- customer ERP portal;
- inventory visibility;

**“Here are our products.”**

and more like:

> **“Tell us what water system you operate, what is going wrong, or what you need to achieve. C-Water connects the engineering, chemistry, filtration, instrumentation, control, and service required to solve it.”**

That is the central product idea.

The strongest combined experience from the four brands is:

**C-Water**
→ local relationship + engineering + service

**TIMEX**
→ filtration

**Kurita**
→ chemistry + optimization + water/process treatment

**Walchem**
→ sensing + dosing + control + digital monitoring

Together:

### **Diagnose → Design → Treat → Control → Monitor → Optimize**

This should become the conceptual backbone of the entire website.
