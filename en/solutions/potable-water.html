01Why It Matters

Potable Water

In most buildings the treatment plant is not where potable quality is lost — the storage tank and the distribution network are. Residence time, temperature, stagnant branches and inadequate disinfectant residual do more damage than the incoming supply.

02Common Problems

What Goes Wrong.

  • Turbidity and particulates

    Particles shield organisms from disinfection and affect both appearance and taste.

  • Inconsistent disinfectant residual

    Residual decays with time and temperature. A correct dose at the plant is not a correct residual at the far end of the network.

  • Storage and stagnation

    Oversized tanks and low-demand branches increase residence time and give growth an opportunity.

  • Taste, odour and hardness

    Acceptability matters. Water that meets a standard but is not drunk has not solved the problem.

03Operational Risks

What It Costs the Operation.

Public health exposure

The consequence of failure in a potable system is categorically different from a utility system.

Regulatory and audit findings

Records and continuous evidence are usually required, not just periodic test certificates.

Guest and occupant confidence

In hospitality and healthcare, water quality is visible to the customer.

04The C-Water Approach

How We Work the Problem.

  1. 01

    Verify the source

    Full analysis of the incoming supply across its normal range, including microbiological indicators.

  2. 02

    Establish barriers

    Filtration and disinfection sized so that no single failure removes all protection.

  3. 03

    Control the residual

    Dosing controlled against a measured residual, verified at the far point of the network rather than at the plant.

  4. 04

    Manage storage

    Turnover, temperature and tank condition reviewed alongside the treatment itself.

  5. 05

    Record continuously

    Logged online measurement provides the evidence trail that periodic sampling cannot.
