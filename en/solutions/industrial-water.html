01Why It Matters

Industrial Water

On a site with a cooling system, a boiler house, a process water plant and an effluent plant, the four are usually managed as four contracts. They are one water balance. Blowdown from one is often a candidate feed for another, and a change in make-up quality affects all of them at once.

02Common Problems

What Goes Wrong.

  • Systems managed in isolation

    Separate suppliers, separate programmes and separate reporting hide the interactions between systems.

  • No site water balance

    Without knowing where water enters, concentrates and leaves, reduction opportunities are invisible.

  • Unrealised reuse

    Streams that could feed a lower-grade duty are discharged because nobody has mapped the qualities against the demands.

  • Fragmented technical support

    When something crosses system boundaries, no single supplier owns the answer.

03Operational Risks

What It Costs the Operation.

Compounded operating cost

Inefficiency in each system adds up across water, chemical, energy and maintenance.

Production availability

Utilities failures stop production regardless of which utility failed.

Water security

Sites with constrained or variable supply carry that risk into every system.

04The C-Water Approach

How We Work the Problem.

  1. 01

    Map the water balance

    Every intake, transfer, concentration point and discharge across the site, quantified.

  2. 02

    Assess each system

    Condition, control quality and treatment performance judged individually and then against each other.

  3. 03

    Prioritise by impact

    Not everything is worth doing at once. The sequence follows risk and return, not system size.

  4. 04

    Standardise control

    Common instrumentation and reporting across systems makes the site legible to the people running it.

  5. 05

    Review as one

    A single technical review across all systems catches what four separate reports do not.
