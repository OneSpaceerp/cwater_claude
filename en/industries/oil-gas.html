Oil & Gas

Water Treatment for Demanding Environments.

Oil and gas water duties combine high solids, variable oil content, aggressive chemistry and often remote locations. Equipment selection has to survive the duty, and the support model has to survive the distance.

Talk to an EngineerTalk to an Engineer

01Typical Water Systems

What Is Usually on Site.

  • Produced water treatment

    Oil removal, solids separation and conditioning before disposal or reuse.

  • Injection water

    Fine filtration and chemical conditioning to protect formation and injection equipment.

  • Utility cooling

    Process cooling under high ambient temperatures and often with limited make-up water.

  • Utility and potable supply

    Site water for personnel and services, frequently from a treated or brackish source.

02Industry Water Challenges

What Makes It Difficult.

  • High and variable solids

    Filtration has to handle a load that changes with the well, not a design average.

  • Oil carryover

    Free and emulsified oil blinds filtration media and disrupts downstream treatment.

  • Corrosive chemistry

    Chloride, H₂S and dissolved gases make material selection as important as treatment.

  • Remote operation

    Sites with limited attendance need equipment that runs unattended and reports when it cannot.

03Operational Risks

What Is at Stake.

Formation damage

Injection water quality has consequences that are expensive and slow to reverse.

Discharge limits

Produced water disposal is closely regulated and monitored.

Production uptime

Water handling capacity can become the constraint on production rate.
